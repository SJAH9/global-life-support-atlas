Project background

Life encloses carbon.

The Global Life-Support Atlas is an open research project in private development. It models regional resilience through the systems that keep life possible rather than reducing the field to emissions alone.

Origins and purpose

The project originates in the causal framework developed in Causality and Attraction and Nested Causal Modelling. Climate science is enclosed by measurement networks, global models, political treaties, moral language, energy systems, and public fear. It encloses agriculture, local ecology, urban heat, industrial production, water, and biological adaptation. The project’s active departure is the reduction of that life-support continuum to a politically manageable carbon frame.

The atlas tests whether water, food, heat, soil, energy, transport, health, ecological integrity, and institutional capacity predict harm and adaptation more usefully than carbon measures alone. Carbon remains inside the model. Climate funding is not presumed unnecessary; the project asks whether it can be aimed toward a fuller field of life.

Development and participation

The repository remains private until a launch date is approved. At launch, public participation can contribute source-bearing local observations and corrections through a review queue. Community reports never become scored evidence merely by submission.

Principal Investigator

Sid J.A. Hubbard is the Principal Investigator. The initial atlas is written, directed, and engineered by its Principal Investigator with AI and data-science tools. Scope, source acceptance, scoring, corrections, and publication decisions remain with the Principal Investigator.
