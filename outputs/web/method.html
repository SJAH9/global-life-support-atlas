Nested Causal Modelling

Resilience is a system property.

The atlas asks whether a region can preserve life-support functions through stress, response, recovery, and adaptation. It does not substitute a single inventory for that question.

E(life-support conditions) [ D(observed departure) | E(harm, response, and adaptation) ]

Nine nested domains

Water

Availability, access, stress, storage, and recovery.

Food

Availability, access, nutrition, and stability.

Heat

Exposure, extremes, urban amplification, and adaptation.

Soil

Carbon, chemistry, water retention, erosion, and function.

Energy

Access, reliability, intensity, and essential service continuity.

Transport

Access and movement of people, food, water, and medicine.

Health

Burden, prevention, care capacity, and recovery.

Ecological integrity

Function, connectivity, protection, and adaptive capacity.

Institutional capacity

Observation, coordination, accountability, and correction.

Scoring contract

Indicators are normalized only under a published direction, lower and upper reference bound, unit, year, source version, and provenance status. Indicators average within a domain. The nine domains combine through a geometric mean so a severe weakness cannot be fully canceled by abundance elsewhere. No composite score is issued until every domain is represented.

Comparison test

The research design compares life-support enclosure with narrower carbon measures against independently specified harm and adaptation outcomes. Predictive performance, uncertainty, coverage, lag, and sensitivity to normalization must be published together. A favorable result is not assumed.

Carbon’s address

Carbon appears inside energy and industrial production and connects outward to heat, water, food, soil, ecology, transport, health, and institutions. Keeping carbon inside the model preserves its causal relevance without treating it as the full boundary of life.
